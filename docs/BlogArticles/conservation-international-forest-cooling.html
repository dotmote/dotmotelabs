Conservation International Highlights Our Research on Forest Cooling

Aji John December 2024

How much can forests cool the places where plants and wildlife live during an extreme heat event? Conservation International explored that question in its feature, “How cool are forests? A hot new study has answers.” We’re glad to see our research receive this recognition and reach a wider conservation audience.

What our study found

Our 2024 study, published in Environmental Research Communications, examined forest microclimates during the 2021 Pacific Northwest heat dome. We found that forest understories were, on average, 3°C cooler than nearby clearcut areas and 4°C cooler than regional averages during the event. Denser canopy cover provided stronger buffering, and the cooling effect varied through the forest’s vertical layers.

These findings show why measuring conditions within forests matters: regional weather data alone cannot capture the cooler microclimates that forest structure can create. Understanding those differences can help researchers and conservation practitioners evaluate forests as refuges during extreme heat.
